Loan Intent and Stability

Analyzing purpose and history length

Loan Intent (loan_intent):

Highest Risk Intents: Debt Consolidation and Medical loans often show

elevated risk, suggesting they may be issued during periods of financial stress.

Lowest Risk Intents: Education and Home Improvement tend to be safer.

Credit History Length (cb_person_cred_hist_length):

Loans issued with a credit history of 2-3 years have a default rate of up to

35%. Loans with a history of 10+ years have a stable default rate under 15%.

Proposed Mitigation Strategies

Translating data into policy

1. Enforce LTI (Loan-to-Income) Cap

Action: Implement a hard upper limit on loan_percent_income at 25% for

all new applications, irrespective of credit grade.

2. Minimum Credit History

Action: Require a minimum cb_person_cred_hist_length of 5 years for

Grade D-G loans to ensure stability.

3. Segmented Interest Rate Review

Action: Review and adjust interest rates for the highest-risk loan intents

(Debt Consolidation, Medical) that fall into Grade C or D.

Conclusion and Recommendations

Summary of key insights

Summary of Key Findings:

Grade is King: Risk correlates almost perfectly with loan_grade and

loan_int_rate.

LTI is a Breaker: The 25% Loan-to-Income ratio threshold is the strongest

actionable policy point.

Combined Risk: Young, renting borrowers with short credit histories form

the highest-risk segment.
